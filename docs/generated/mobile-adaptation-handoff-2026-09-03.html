Mobile adaptationWhat the demo shows, and what the product must do

A handoff for product managers preparing Ask Nanci for a phone.

This page describes a demo. The product is built in another codebase, so the rules here transfer and the code does not.

Principles

Three principles govern the rules in this document.

Each rule in the sections below follows from one of them. Where a case is not covered by a rule, the principle decides it. Sources for all three are listed under Resources at the end of this page.

Every control is built for a thumb. A thumb covers more screen than a pointer and has no hover state. Targets need space around them, not only size.
Nothing is hidden by accident. If there is more content past the edge, the screen shows it. Moving something out of the way on purpose is fine. Hiding it by accident is not.
Nothing you read or tap gets smaller. The layout gives way instead, stacking or scrolling to fit. Text keeps whatever size the reader has set on their device.

Typography

One scale serves both screens. What is still unsettled is who owns how a block of text reads, and how small text is allowed to get.

Requirements

Demo shows One type scale for both screens. Interface text shares it and stays readable at phone width A second scale doubles every typography decision
Demo shows Inputs never zoom the page. Every field a reader types into is at least 16px iOS zooms into any smaller field on focus, and the reader pinches back out every time
Decide An answer's style is owned in one place. Not element by element Changing how answers read is one edit instead of dozens
Decide No text fixed in px below the scale. The smallest labels follow the reader's font setting 153 labels ignore it today; a reader who enlarged their font gets nothing

Sizing

Controls are drawn for a pointer and touched by a thumb. The two can be reconciled without redrawing anything.

Requirements

Mobile-Specific Behaviors

Two surfaces change shape on a phone. A third was changed and changed back, which is the more useful lesson.

Charts

A chart resizes itself. What it does not do is decide what to drop, and that decision belongs in one place rather than in every chart.

Requirements
State Requirement Why it matters
Demo shows A chart fits any width. It redraws at its container's size A fixed-width chart is the first thing to overflow a phone
Demo shows Axis labels thin out on their own. Nothing forces every label to show Forced labels read fine on a laptop and collide on a phone
Demo shows Touch works. Tooltips answer a tap, ranges drag Hover does not exist on a phone
Demo shows One phone mode, set once for every chart. Margins, label thinning and legend placement Set chart by chart, one of them is always wrong
Demo shows Long labels have room. A turned month name is not clipped The default margin is enough for a number and not for a word
Demo shows The legend sits below the chart as ordinary content. It never eats into the plot A legend inside the chart wraps on a phone and the chart cannot make room for it

Table

A wide table on a phone always scrolls. The question is whether a reader can tell, and whether they can still see which row they are reading.

Requirements
State Requirement Why it matters
Demo shows A wide table scrolls sideways inside its own frame. Columns keep a readable width Squeezing columns to fit makes every cell wrap into a column of words
Demo shows An edge fade says columns continue. A table cut off cleanly at the screen edge looks finished, and the reader never scrolls
Demo shows One shared table with one density. Built individually, the demo's tables drifted individually
Demo shows Every table goes through the shared one. Five risk-console tables were converted to it A table built on its own gets none of the rules above
Decide The column that identifies the row stays pinned. Never an index or a checkbox Scroll sideways and the name leaves the screen, so the figures belong to nobody

Tab and Segmented Controls

Resources

Published guidance behind the three principles.

Touch Targets on Touchscreens, Nielsen Norman Group. A target of at least 1cm, because a thumb covers about 2.5cm, and spacing that matters as much as size.
The Illusion of Completeness, Nielsen Norman Group. Why a screen that looks finished stops the reader, and what signals that more is there.
Beware Horizontal Scrolling, Nielsen Norman Group. What people miss when content runs off the edge, and why a cue shown only on hover reaches nobody.
Defer Secondary Content When Writing for Mobile Users, Nielsen Norman Group. Layering content by priority rather than cutting it.
Progressive Disclosure, Nielsen Norman Group. Deferring on purpose, which is the difference between layered and hidden.
Reflow, Success Criterion 1.4.10, Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. Content at 320px with no sideways scrolling and nothing lost.
